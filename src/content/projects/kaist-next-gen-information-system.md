## Overview

KAIST ran a multi-year, institution-wide program — approximately **USD 130M** — to replace its
aging administrative systems with a single next-generation integrated information system.
I was a **core working-level member for the academic domain**: the systems that run the entire
student lifecycle, from admission to graduation.

## The academic domain

Over five years I built a deep, detailed command of how KAIST’s academic operations actually
work, and carried that domain knowledge directly into the new system’s design:

- **Curriculum (교과과정)** — program and degree requirements, course catalog, prerequisites.
- **Classes (수업)** — course registration, scheduling, enrollment, and grading.
- **Graduation assessment (졸업사정)** — automated requirement checking and graduation certification.
- **Role-based permissions (사용자별 권한)** — fine-grained access across students, faculty, and staff.

## What I did

- **Design.** Was deeply involved in designing the academic module — data models, business
  rules, and workflows that faithfully encode real academic policy and its many edge cases.
- **Development verification & testing.** Drove verification and testing of the delivered
  module, validating behavior against requirements and hardening it against boundary conditions.
- **Deployment.** Saw the module through **production deployment to a successful launch**, then
  stabilized it in operation.
- **Continuity.** Kept mission-critical academic services — registration, grades, graduation —
  reliable throughout the migration from the legacy platform.

## Outcome

- Delivered the academic component of a **~USD 130M** institution-wide modernization, on track
  and in production.
- Recognized with a **Special Commendation from KAIST (2025)** for the build.
