## Overview

Over **three years**, KAIST ran an institution-wide program — approximately **USD 130M** — to
replace its aging administrative systems with a single next-generation integrated information
system. I was a **core working-level member for the academic domain**: the systems that run the
entire student lifecycle, from admission to graduation.

## The academic domain

Across five years of operations I built a deep, detailed command of how KAIST’s academics
actually work, and carried that domain knowledge directly into the new system’s design:

- **Curriculum (교과과정)** — program and degree requirements, course catalog, prerequisites.
- **Classes (수업)** — course registration, scheduling, enrollment, and grading.
- **Graduation assessment (졸업사정)** — automated requirement checking and certification.
- **Role-based permissions (사용자별 권한)** — fine-grained access across students, faculty, and staff.

## What I did

- **Design.** Was deeply involved in designing the academic module — data models, business
  rules, and workflows that faithfully encode real academic policy and its many edge cases.
- **Requirements modernization.** Reviewed and re-based **1,400+ common and major-specific
  graduation requirements** across departments (CS, EE, Mechanical, and more) onto the
  next-generation system, resolving long-standing policy–system mismatches.
- **Assessment logic.** Redesigned graduation-assessment exception handling and the logic for
  internships, special lectures, and substitute courses; improved thesis-review management
  (proposal, defense, external committees), eliminating recurring errors.
- **Verification & deployment.** Drove verification and testing, then saw the module through
  **production deployment to a successful launch** and stabilized it in operation.

## Impact

- Ran the transition-aligned graduation season **without disruption — 2,000+ graduations and
  degree conferrals processed**.
- Built new **degree certificate / name / number issuance** features, improving academic-record
  accuracy and administrative trust while easing the load on partner departments.
- Recognized with a **Special Commendation from KAIST (2025)** for the build.
